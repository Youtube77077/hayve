# Getting Hayve's first users

Hayve is a marketplace on several sides at once: buyers need sellers, riders need drivers, eaters
need restaurants. The rule that decides everything below: **launch one city, fill the supply side
first, and only then invite buyers, so nobody opens an empty app.**

## 1. Pick one launch city and one "hero" service

- Choose one city where you can personally reach people (US first: e.g. one New York borough or one
  mid-sized college town). Everything else in the app stays visible but labelled "coming soon".
- Pick one hero service to lead with. The best first one is the one with the least supply needed and
  the most frequent use: **Shop (buy and sell second-hand) + Pay (split bills)**. Both work with zero
  restaurants or drivers and both spread person to person.
- Food, rides and stays come second, once you have people opening the app weekly.

## 2. Supply before demand (weeks 1–4)

| Side | How to get the first 50 | What you give them |
|---|---|---|
| Sellers | Message people already selling on local Facebook groups, Vinted and Marktplaats-style sites; campus sell groups | Zero seller fees for 6 months, featured on Home, "Sold in 2 hours" cards to post |
| Restaurants | Walk in to 20 independent places near the launch area at quiet times (2–4 pm) | Lower commission than the big apps for the first year, payouts the next day |
| Drivers / couriers | Drivers who already drive for Uber/Lyft/DoorDash (they multi-app), local bike-courier groups | Keep 85%, instant payout to the Hayve balance, ID check in a minute |
| Hosts | Small hosts with 1–3 listings on Airbnb; they can sync calendars | Lower guest fees so their place is cheaper on Hayve |

Use the admin import with each partner's permission to put their existing listings on Hayve in
minutes, so joining costs them nothing.

## 3. Private beta with a waitlist (weeks 3–8)

- A simple waitlist page on the website: email + city. Show "You're number 312. Move up by inviting
  friends" with a personal invite link.
- Let people in by invite code only, in batches of 50–100 per week, so supply keeps up.
- Every new member gets 3 invite codes. Invites are the main way in.

## 4. Built-in reasons to share (the product does the marketing)

These are moments people actually like posting, because they make them look good:

| Moment | Status | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Holiday trip card** | Built | Your photo, the route you drove, places visited, "Hayve Holiday · Everything in one app", short link |
| **Split a bill / payment link** | Built | Friends without Hayve pay by card through the link, then see "Get Hayve" |
| **Anonymous wishlist gifts** | Built | Friends gift from your public wishlist without you knowing who |
| "Sold in 2 hours" card for sellers | Next | Item photo, time to sell, earnings (optional) |
| "Hayve Year" recap (December) | Next | Rides taken, places visited, money earned selling, favourite restaurant |
| First-ride / first-order cards | Next | Small, story-sized, with the invite link |

Every card carries a short link that opens the onboarding with the sharer's invite already applied.

## 5. Referrals that pay both sides

- Both people get money back in their Hayve balance after the new person's **first completed**
  order or ride (not at sign-up). Example: $5 each, max 20 invites a month per person.
- Abuse limits: one reward per device and payment card, rewards held 7 days, no rewards for
  self-invites.
- Show each person how many friends they've brought in (and what they've earned).

## 6. Measure from day one

Track these weekly (with consent where required):
- Onboarding: drop-off per step (the progress bar steps), and how many reach their first action.
- Activation: % of new members who complete one order, ride, sale or payment in 7 days.
- Retention: % active in week 4 and week 8.
- Referral rate (K-factor): invites accepted per active member.
- Supply health: listings per active buyer, restaurant wait time, driver pickup time.

## 7. Content that works for this app

- Short videos of the app doing something in one place that normally needs four apps
  ("I sold my jacket, paid my friend back and booked a ride home in one app").
- Seller success stories from the launch city.
- The holiday trip cards people post (repost them, with permission).

## 8. What not to do yet

- No paid ads until week-4 retention is healthy; ads into an empty city waste money.
- Don't launch every service everywhere at once.
- No fake reviews or fake listings in the public app (fine in private demos you show yourself).

## First 30 days, as a checklist

1. Choose the city and hero service.
2. Sign up 50 sellers and 10 restaurants in that city (import their listings with permission).
3. Put up the waitlist with invite links.
4. Let in the first 100 people; watch the onboarding numbers daily.
5. Fix the biggest drop-off, then let in the next 200.
